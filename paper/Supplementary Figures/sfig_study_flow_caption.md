# Figure caption — primary sample inclusion (Snow et al. polygons)

**Figure. Sample inclusion for the primary analysis.** The survey registry contains 124 DHS and MIS surveys and 93 MICS surveys in 43 countries. 46 MICS surveys have no complete birth history and 5 surveys are excluded because Lesotho is malaria free and the Malaria Atlas Project publishes no prevalence surface for it, leaving 166 processed surveys in 40 countries. Counts below the registry refer to child–age-band records, not unique children.

Among 10,657,524 band entries within the 60 months before interview, 1,347,434 have an incomplete potential band and 418,661 have entry years outside 2000–2024, leaving 8,891,429 eligible records. Each record is placed in the Snow et al. (2017) malaria-endemic polygons through its smallest reported administrative unit: the polygon holding at least 90% of that unit's population, or else the union of the polygons each holding at least 5%. 261,123 records are not placed: 114,205 in survey-regions with no Snow polygon (the Comoros, Zanzibar and São Tomé and Príncipe), 75,588 whose survey-region could not be matched to a boundary, and 71,330 in malaria-free or island units or in units more than half malaria free. 8,630,306 records remain in 1,715 survey × unit areas; 990,863 of them lack at least one required covariate (first missing: wasting 305,014; facility delivery 207,325; health expenditure 185,355; political stability 178,922; electricity 83,796; wealth score 30,451), leaving 7,639,443 records and 105,114 deaths in 136 surveys from 37 countries.

30 processed surveys contribute no records to the final sample. The final records represent 2,335,868 distinct children.

Seven completed-month bands are analysed separately: <1, 1–5, 6–11, 12–23, 24–35, 36–47 and 48–59. The outcome is death during the band. Annual MAP PfPR₂–₁₀ of the record's unit (population-weighted over its polygons) and the national covariates are assigned at band-entry year. The 17 adjustment variables comprise survey × unit summaries of maternal age at first birth, education, wealth, urban residence, DTP3/measles coverage, facility delivery, short birth interval, improved water, improved sanitation, electricity, wasting and stunting, computed from the DHS and MICS recodes, plus national annual log child HIV incidence, log GDP per capita, log health expenditure per capita and political stability. For South Sudan (independent from July 2011), GDP per capita, health expenditure per capita and political stability for band-entry years before 2011 are Sudan's values for the same year (2005–2010: 57,509 records, 955 deaths); its child HIV incidence is its own series.
Counts are read from the saved build, eligibility, placement, unit and selection ledgers and reconciled with the seven age-band fits. See [counts](flow_counts.csv) and [survey selection](survey_selection.csv).
