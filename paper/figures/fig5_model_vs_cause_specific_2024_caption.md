# Figure 5 caption — PfPR-ACM against cause-specific malaria mortality, by country

**Figure. Malaria-attributable under-five mortality in 2024: PfPR-ACM model against cause-specific estimates.** Each point is a country; rates are deaths per 1,000 child-years under 5. (A) PfPR-ACM estimates using UN IGME all-cause mortality (y) against UN IGME (CA-CODE) malaria deaths (x), both over the UN WPP population under 5. (B) PfPR-ACM estimates using IHME all-cause mortality (y) against IHME malaria deaths (x), both over IHME person-years. The PfPR-ACM estimate counts all deaths averted if prevalence fell to zero (direct and indirect); the cause-specific estimates count deaths assigned to malaria as the underlying cause. The dashed line is equality; countries are coloured by UN M49 sub-region (Middle Africa as Central Africa). Countries where the PfPR-ACM estimate is more than 2.5 per 1,000 above the cause-specific estimate or more than 0.6 below it are named. Spearman correlation: 0.91 (A) and 0.91 (B); the PfPR-ACM estimate is higher in 31 (A) and 30 (B) of 42 countries. Vertical lines are 95% uncertainty intervals of the PfPR-ACM estimates, from 500 joint draws of MAP's national PfPR[2–10], the all-cause deaths and the PfPR–death curves (Supplementary Methods); horizontal lines are the published intervals of the cause-specific estimates (the 90% bounds of the UN IGME (CA-CODE) malaria deaths in A, and IHME's 95% uncertainty intervals in B). The lower bound exceeds the cause-specific estimate in 17 (A) and 17 (B) countries, and the upper bound is below it in 4 (A) and 5 (B). Intervals beyond the axes are truncated with an arrow: South Sudan in A (upper bound 15.4). CA-CODE takes malaria from WHO estimates in 13 low-burden countries; South Sudan's IHME all-cause mortality is its crisis-free value.
