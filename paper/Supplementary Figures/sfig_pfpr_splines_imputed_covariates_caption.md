# Supplementary figure caption: imputed-covariate sensitivity (DHS and MICS)

Sensitivity of the fitted PfPR–mortality relationship to the treatment of missing covariates. Log mortality hazard ratios relative to PfPR[2–10] = 20% by completed-month age band, with pointwise 95% conditional intervals shaded, from the PfPR-ACM model fitted to the complete-case primary sample (7,807,872 child–age-band records, 108,796 deaths, 136 surveys in 37 countries) and to every record placed in a Snow et al. unit after imputing all remaining covariate gaps (8,630,306 records, 122,092 deaths, 161 surveys in 38 countries, including all five Malaria Indicator Surveys and all 43 MICS surveys with complete birth histories outside Lesotho, the Comoros and São Tomé and Príncipe, which have no Snow et al. malaria-endemic polygon). Whole-survey gaps in the regional indicators (wasting, stunting, facility delivery, electricity and the wealth score) were imputed by chained equations at the survey × unit level (predictive mean matching, 10 imputations, point value their mean); the missing 2001 World Governance Indicators round by linear interpolation; health expenditure for Zimbabwe 2000–2009, Somalia 2000–2012 and all countries in 2024 from a generalised additive model on the observed national panel; Liberia's child HIV incidence from UNAIDS counts of new infections among children (São Tomé and Príncipe, the only country without an incidence series, has no Snow et al. unit). In both analyses, South Sudan (independent from July 2011) takes Sudan's observed GDP per capita, health expenditure per capita and political stability for band-entry years before 2011. Values used by the complete-case analysis were never replaced. The specification, reference knots, basis dimensions and gamma = 2 are those of the primary analysis; covariates were re-standardised on the enlarged sample. Each curve is drawn over the central 95% of its own exposure distribution. Hazard ratios for PfPR 40% to 20% are 0.96, 0.92, 0.85, 0.89, 0.91, 0.89, 0.95 (complete case) and 0.96, 0.93, 0.85, 0.89, 0.90, 0.89, 0.96 (imputed) for the seven bands from youngest to oldest. Refitting the imputed version in each of the 10 imputed datasets and pooling with Rubin's rules changed these hazard ratios by at most 0.001; the between-imputation share of interval variance was at most 1.0%. The two samples are nested, so the comparison is descriptive.
