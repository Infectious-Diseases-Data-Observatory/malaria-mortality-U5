# Supplementary figure caption — PfPR-ACM against UN IGME malaria mortality, by country, log scale

**Figure. Malaria-attributable under-five mortality in 2024: PfPR-ACM model against UN IGME malaria deaths, on log scales.** Each point is a country; rates are deaths per 1,000 child-years under 5. PfPR-ACM estimates using UN IGME all-cause mortality (y) against UN IGME (CA-CODE) malaria deaths (x), both over the UN WPP population under 5. The PfPR-ACM estimate counts all deaths averted if prevalence fell to zero (direct and indirect); UN IGME counts deaths assigned to malaria as the underlying cause. The dashed line is equality. Point area is proportional to the population under 5 (UN WPP). The data are those of the main-text figure; every country is named. Spearman correlation: 0.91; the PfPR-ACM estimate is higher in 31 of 42 countries. Vertical lines are 95% uncertainty intervals of the PfPR-ACM estimates, from 500 joint draws of MAP's national PfPR[2–10], the all-cause deaths and the PfPR–death curves (Supplementary Methods); horizontal lines are the 90% bounds of the UN IGME (CA-CODE) malaria deaths. The lower bound exceeds the UN IGME estimate in 17 countries, and the upper bound is below it in 4. Both axes are logarithmic and start at 0.1 per 1,000: a value below that is drawn at 0.1 with an open circle (Zimbabwe: UN IGME 0.23, PfPR-ACM 0.08; Namibia: UN IGME 0.11, PfPR-ACM 0.02; Gabon: UN IGME 0.09, PfPR-ACM 1.11; Botswana: UN IGME 0.00, PfPR-ACM 0.00; Eswatini: UN IGME 0.01, PfPR-ACM 0.00), and intervals are cut there. CA-CODE assigns malaria deaths to ages 1–59 months as a whole and none under 1 month, and takes malaria from WHO estimates in 13 low-burden countries.
