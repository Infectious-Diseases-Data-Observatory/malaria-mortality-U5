# Figure caption — primary sample inclusion (Snow et al. polygons)

**Figure. Sample inclusion for the primary analysis.** Of 217 DHS, MIS and MICS surveys conducted in sub-Saharan Africa since 2000 (119 DHS, 5 MIS, 93 MICS; 43 countries), 171 recorded complete birth histories (119 DHS, 5 MIS, 47 MICS; 41 countries); the other 46 MICS surveys recorded only summary birth histories. 35 surveys were excluded: 5 from Lesotho, which is malaria free and has no Malaria Atlas Project prevalence surface; 5 from Comoros and São Tomé and Príncipe, which lie outside the Snow et al. (2017) polygons; and 25 (14 DHS, 5 MIS, 6 MICS) in which at least one required covariate was unavailable in every survey × unit area. 136 surveys (99 DHS, 37 MICS) from 37 countries were included.

In the included surveys, 9,241,771 child age-band records were entered within the 60 months before interview; counts from here are records, not unique children. 1,242,296 were excluded because the band could not be completed by the interview (1,167,873) or began outside 2000–2024 (74,423); 162,810 because the record's area could not be placed in a Snow et al. polygon (66,826 in survey-regions not matched to a boundary; 36,290 in Zanzibar, which has no polygon) or lay in malaria-free or island units or in units more than half malaria free (59,694); and 35,880 because at least one required covariate was unavailable (first missing: health expenditure 35,880). Each record is placed in the polygons through its smallest reported administrative unit: the polygon holding at least 90% of that unit's population, or else the union of the polygons each holding at least 5%. The primary sample has 7,800,785 records and 108,657 deaths from 2,344,504 children of 1,126,610 mothers (complete birth histories), in 1,452 survey × unit areas.

Record counts over all 166 processed surveys, including the excluded ones, are in the full ledger: 10,657,524 band entries, 8,891,429 eligible records, 8,630,306 placed in 1,715 survey × unit areas.

Seven completed-month bands are analysed separately: <1, 1–5, 6–11, 12–23, 24–35, 36–47 and 48–59. The outcome is death during the band. Annual MAP PfPR₂–₁₀ of the record's unit (population-weighted over its polygons) and the national covariates are assigned at band-entry year. The 17 adjustment variables comprise survey × unit summaries of maternal age at first birth, education, wealth, urban residence, DTP3/measles coverage, facility delivery, short birth interval, improved water, improved sanitation, electricity, wasting and stunting, computed from the DHS and MICS recodes, plus national annual log child HIV incidence, log GDP per capita, log health expenditure per capita and political stability. For South Sudan (independent from July 2011), GDP per capita, health expenditure per capita and political stability for band-entry years before 2011 are Sudan's values for the same year (2005–2010: 57,509 records, 955 deaths); its child HIV incidence is its own series.
Counts are read from the saved build, eligibility, placement, unit and selection ledgers and reconciled with the seven age-band fits. See [counts](flow_counts.csv) and [survey selection](survey_selection.csv).
