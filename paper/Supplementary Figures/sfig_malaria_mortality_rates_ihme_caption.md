# Figure caption — national malaria-attributable under-five mortality, IHME

**Figure. Malaria prevalence and under-five mortality in 42 sub-Saharan African countries, 2000–2024, with all-cause mortality from IHME.** (A) National MAP PfPR[2–10], population-weighted using a fixed GPW 2020 spatial distribution (the same in both versions of this figure). (B) All-cause under-five deaths per 1,000 child-years, with crisis years replaced (below). (C) Malaria-attributable under-five deaths per 1,000 child-years: for each of the seven age bands of the PfPR-ACM model, the band's attributable fraction 1 − exp[f_g(0) − f_g(P)] at the national PfPR[2–10] times the band's all-cause deaths, summed over bands and divided by the same child-years. All-cause deaths are IHME GBD estimates by age (0–6 days and 7–27 days combined for the <1 month band; 1–5 months, 6–11 months and 12–23 months directly; 2–4 years divided equally across the three annual bands), and child-years those implied by IHME's under-five deaths and rates. Crisis years, in which the IHME all-cause under-five death rate is more than 25% above its crisis-free trend (the log-linear interpolation between the nearest non-crisis years), take that trend in every age band, because the attributable fraction of crisis deaths need not resemble that of other years: Somalia 2011; South Sudan 2024 (62,581 deaths removed in all). Thin lines are countries, coloured by UN M49 sub-region (Middle Africa as Central Africa); the black line is the population-weighted average over the 42 countries (PfPR[2–10] weighted by the UN WPP population under 5; mortality rates weighted by child-years, that is total deaths divided by total child-years). The average malaria-attributable rate fell from 10.2 to 3.3 deaths per 1,000 child-years between 2000 and 2024 (29.7% and 22.3% of all-cause under-five deaths). Panels B and C use the same axis limits in both versions. Point estimates only; zero prevalence lies below the observed exposure support, so the counterfactual requires extrapolation.
