# Main-paper figures and draft captions

Figures contain no title, subtitle or embedded caption. Axis labels, age/year facet labels and legends remain. File numbering here does not alter the order of figures in TeX.

## Figure 1 — complete birth histories used, by analysis unit

File: `fig1_analysis_units_map.png` (source `analysis_units_map/analysis_units_map.png`; values in `analysis_units_map/analysis_units_by_polygon.csv`).

Complete birth histories used in the primary analysis, by sub-national polygon of Snow et al. (2017). Fill: interviewed mothers with at least one child-band record in the analysis, summed over the 136 included surveys (99 DHS and 37 MICS; interviews 2003–2025) in 37 countries; in total 1,125,610 mothers contribute 7,639,443 child-band records and 105,114 deaths. Records are placed in the polygon holding at least 90% of the GPW 2020 population of their reported area, otherwise in the malaria-endemic polygons each holding at least 5% (malaria-free parts are left out, and areas more than half malaria free are dropped). In 215 of the 1,452 survey × area units this gives a union of two or more polygons; the 161,042 mothers (14.3%) in these unions are divided among their polygons by population share. The 465 polygons with data hold a median of 1,715 mothers (range 76–49,763) from a median of 4 surveys (range 1–11).

Light grey: the 55 malaria-endemic polygons without data, 53 in the 6 countries that have malaria-endemic polygons but no included survey (Botswana, Djibouti, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Somalia and Sudan), 1 in Mauritania and 1 in Namibia. Warm grey: the 29 malaria-free and 2 island polygons (Bioko, Annobón), not analysed. Pale: North Africa and Western Sahara, and islands without a polygon such as the Comoros and Zanzibar. Light blue: the 38 lakes and reservoirs of at least 300 km² within the map in Natural Earth (1:10m), drawn over the polygons and national borders. Slivers between the polygons and the national boundaries take the class of the adjacent polygon. White lines: polygon boundaries; black lines: national land borders of the sub-Saharan countries. Africa Albers equal-area projection (ESRI:102022); the scale bar is approximate.

Survey years, inclusion and exclusions by country are shown in the supplementary survey map and timing figure.

## Figure 2 — hazard ratio of death by age band, relative to zero prevalence

File: `fig2_hazard_ratio_by_age.png` (source `hazard_ratio_by_age/fig2_hazard_ratio_by_age.png`; values in `hazard_ratio_by_age/hazard_ratio_curves.csv`).

**Figure. Malaria prevalence and age-specific all-cause mortality.** Hazard ratio of death in each age band at national PfPR[2–10] relative to zero prevalence, from the seven age-band models of the PfPR-ACM model. Lines are point estimates and shaded areas pointwise 95% confidence intervals (conditional on the estimated smoothing parameters). Curves are solid over the central 95% of each band's exposure distribution and dashed from zero to its 2.5th percentile (about 2%), where the zero-prevalence reference is reached by extrapolation. The attributable fraction at prevalence P is 1 − 1/HR(P). For example, at 40% prevalence the hazard ratio is 1.11 (1.04–1.19) for neonates and 2.46 (2.14–2.82) at 24–35 months.

## Figure 3 — malaria-attributable fraction by age

File: `fig3_malaria_attributable_fraction_by_age.png` (source `attributable_fraction_by_age.png`).

PfPR-ACM model: estimated share of all-cause deaths attributable to malaria within each completed-month age band, at MAP PfPR[2–10] levels of 10%, 20%, 30% and 40% (lower panel), with the distribution of observed deaths by age band (upper panel). Age is drawn to scale; vertical lines mark the band boundaries at 1, 6, 12, 24, 36 and 48 months. Upper panel: each band's share of all under-five deaths observed in the primary sample, as bars of equal width at the band midpoints (bar width does not show band width). Lower panel: points show 100 × {1 − exp[f_g(0) − f_g(P)]}, using the seven separate primary MAP gamma=2 models, placed near the band midpoint; dashed lines connect discrete age-band estimates and do not imply a continuous-age fit. The denominator is all-cause deaths within each band, not all under-five deaths pooled across bands. The hazard-based attributable fraction follows the primary burden convention of holding annual person-time fixed; no country-specific IHME inputs are used here. Zero prevalence lies below observed exposure support, so the counterfactual requires extrapolation. Vertical bars show conditional pointwise 95% intervals, dodged by prevalence level; the interval limits and support flags are retained in attributable_fraction_by_age.csv.

## Figure 4 — national malaria-attributable under-five mortality with UN IGME all-cause mortality, 2000–2024

File: `fig4_malaria_mortality_rates_unigme.png` (source `mortality_rates/fig_malaria_mortality_rates_unigme.png`; the IHME version is the supplementary figure `sfig_malaria_mortality_rates_ihme.png`).

**Figure. Malaria prevalence and under-five mortality in 42 sub-Saharan African countries, 2000–2024, with all-cause mortality from UN IGME.** (A) National MAP PfPR[2–10], population-weighted using a fixed GPW 2020 spatial distribution (the same in both versions of this figure). (B) All-cause under-five deaths per 1,000 child-years, with crisis years replaced (below). (C) Malaria-attributable under-five deaths per 1,000 child-years: for each of the seven age bands of the PfPR-ACM model, the band's attributable fraction 1 − exp[f_g(0) − f_g(P)] at the national PfPR[2–10] times the band's all-cause deaths, summed over bands and divided by the same child-years. All-cause deaths are UN IGME estimates (UNICEF data warehouse, indicators CME_TMM0, CME_TMM1T11 and CME_TMY1T4) and child-years the UN WPP population under 5 (DM_POP_U5), the population UN IGME itself uses. UN IGME reports deaths at <1 month, 1–11 months and 1–4 years; each group is split across the model's age bands by the primary sample's observed deaths within the group, pooled over countries and years (1–5 months 52.5%, 6–11 months 47.5%, 12–23 months 37.7%, 24–35 months 32.4%, 36–47 months 19.0%, 48–59 months 11.0%). Crisis years, in which the UN IGME all-cause under-five death rate is more than 25% above its crisis-free trend (the log-linear interpolation between the nearest non-crisis years), take that trend in every age band, because the attributable fraction of crisis deaths need not resemble that of other years: Central African Republic 2009, 2014, 2017, 2019, 2021, 2022; Malawi 2001; Somalia 2011, 2022; South Sudan 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018 (689,289 deaths removed in all). Thin lines are countries, coloured by UN M49 sub-region (Middle Africa as Central Africa); the black line is the population-weighted average over the 42 countries (PfPR[2–10] weighted by the UN WPP population under 5; mortality rates weighted by child-years, that is total deaths divided by total child-years). The average malaria-attributable rate fell from 10.6 to 3.5 deaths per 1,000 child-years between 2000 and 2024 (29.5% and 22.4% of all-cause under-five deaths). Panels B and C use the same axis limits in both versions. Point estimates only; zero prevalence lies below the observed exposure support, so the counterfactual requires extrapolation.

## Figure 5 — PfPR-ACM against cause-specific malaria mortality by country, 2024 (UN IGME, A; IHME, B)

File: `fig5_model_vs_cause_specific_2024.png` (source `model_vs_cause_specific/fig5_model_vs_cause_specific_2024.png`; values in `model_vs_cause_specific/figure_data.csv`).

**Figure. Malaria-attributable under-five mortality in 2024: PfPR-ACM model against cause-specific estimates.** Each point is a country; rates are deaths per 1,000 child-years under 5. (A) PfPR-ACM estimates using UN IGME all-cause mortality against UN IGME (CA-CODE) malaria deaths, both over the UN WPP population under 5. (B) PfPR-ACM estimates using IHME all-cause mortality against IHME malaria deaths, both over IHME person-years. The PfPR-ACM estimate counts all deaths averted if prevalence fell to zero (direct and indirect); the cause-specific estimates count deaths assigned to malaria as the underlying cause. The dashed line is equality; countries are coloured by UN M49 sub-region (Middle Africa as Central Africa), and those at 2.5 or more per 1,000 on either axis are labelled with their ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 codes. Spearman correlation: 0.91 (A) and 0.91 (B); the PfPR-ACM estimate is higher in 37 (A) and 34 (B) of 42 countries. CA-CODE takes malaria from WHO estimates in 13 low-burden countries; South Sudan's IHME all-cause mortality is its crisis-free value. Point estimates only.

## Supplementary figure — comparison with IHME and UN IGME as deaths per 1,000 child-years: countries (A), Nigerian states (B) and annual trend (C) (formerly Figure 3)

File: `sfig_burden_comparison.png` (exported to the manuscript's `Supplementary Figures/` folder; source `burden_comparison/fig4_burden_comparison.png`). Panel sources: `annual_comparison/country_estimates_2000_2024.csv` (2024 rates), `nigeria_states/state_totals_2024.csv`, `annual_comparison/figure5_data.csv`. The death-count version is the supplementary figure `burden_comparison/sfig_burden_comparison_counts.png` with its own caption (`CAPTION_counts.md`). The separate three-year country scatter (`burden/country_vs_ihme.png`), state figure (`nigeria_states/`) and annual figure (`annual_comparison/`) remain saved results but are no longer paper figures.

Malaria mortality before age 5 from the PfPR-ACM model compared with IHME and UN IGME cause-specific malaria estimates, expressed as deaths per 1,000 under-five child-years. (A) National rates for 2024 in the 42 countries with both estimates available; each point is a country and the dashed line denotes equality. Each country's model and IHME deaths are divided by the same under-five person-years implied by the IHME all-cause death count and rate. (B) The same comparison for the 36 Nigerian states and the Federal Capital Territory in 2024, using the state person-years implied by the IHME all-cause inputs; colours group states into Nigeria's six geopolitical zones for orientation only. Summed over states the model gives 205,988 deaths against 132,138 IHME malaria deaths (ratio 1.56). (C) Annual under-five malaria mortality, 2000–2024, pooled across the same 42 countries covered by the national burden analysis. For each source the rate is the sum of national under-five malaria deaths divided by the sum of under-five person-years implied by the IHME all-cause death counts and rates, so the three series share one denominator. The UN IGME series is the CA-CODE 2026 release. In each of A and B the two axes are linear and share the same limits, set from the largest rate in that panel (A: 0–9; B: 0–11 deaths per 1,000 child-years). Within each country or state the model and comparator share a denominator, so their ratio is the same as for death counts; the supplementary count version of this figure shows the same comparisons as numbers of deaths. In all panels, PfPR-ACM model deaths equal IHME all-cause deaths in each of seven age bands multiplied by 1 − exp[f_g(0) − f_g(P)], where P is population-weighted MAP PfPR[2–10] for the country or state and year and f_g are the seven fitted age-band effects; IHME early and late neonatal deaths share the <1-month effect and the IHME 2–4 year group is divided equally across the three oldest bands. All values are point estimates; joint uncertainty across countries and age bands is not available and marginal intervals are not summed. Model-attributable all-cause reductions and cause-specific malaria deaths are different estimands, and the model shares IHME all-cause inputs, so agreement is not independent validation.

## Supplementary figure — national PfPR and probability of dying before age 5, all causes and caused by malaria (formerly Figure 4)

File: `sfig_under5_death_probability.png` (exported to the manuscript's `Supplementary Figures/` folder; source `under5_probability/under5_death_probability_2024.png`; values in `under5_probability/under5_death_probability.csv`).

Probability of dying before age 5 in 2024 per 1,000 live births, by country (42 sub-Saharan African countries in the burden comparison, ordered by the probability caused by malaria). Left: national MAP PfPR[2–10] in 2024, population-weighted (GPW 2020, all ages) over MAP-covered pixels, the prevalence at which each country's malaria-caused probability is evaluated (median 16.7%, range 0.1–36.4%). Right: all causes, from IHME age-specific all-cause death rates (light bars), and the part caused by malaria under the PfPR-ACM model (dark bars, with 95% intervals), and the part caused by malaria implied by IHME cause-specific malaria death rates (diamonds, point estimates); all three values are listed on the right. Probabilities are period life-table (synthetic-cohort) calculations, 5q0 = 1 − exp(−Σ_g H_g), over the model's seven age bands (<1, 1–5, 6–11, 12–23, 24–35, 36–47 and 48–59 completed months), with band cumulative hazards H_g from the IHME death rates. The malaria-caused probability is 5q0 minus the probability with malaria transmission removed, for which each band's hazard is multiplied by the model's hazard ratio for PfPR[2–10] = 0 versus the country's current population-weighted MAP prevalence. The IHME value is 5q0 minus the probability with IHME's malaria death rates removed (late neonatal, post-neonatal and 1–4 years; IHME reports no early-neonatal malaria deaths). Across the 42 countries the model's malaria-caused probability is a median 1.50 times IHME's (interquartile range 1.09–2.26) and is higher than IHME's in 34. Intervals draw each band's log hazard ratio independently; they condition on the IHME rates and the fitted smoothing parameters. Zero prevalence lies below the observed exposure support, so the counterfactual is an extrapolation. Against UN IGME under-five mortality for 2024, the IHME-based all-cause probabilities have median ratio 1.07 and correlation 0.84; IHME and UN IGME differ markedly for some countries. The largest malaria-caused probabilities are in South Sudan (33 per 1,000), Sierra Leone (31) and Central African Republic (31); the median is 12 per 1,000, 20% of the all-cause probability.

## Supplementary figure — survey map and timing (formerly Figure 1)

File: `sfig_survey_map_and_timing.png` (exported to the manuscript's `Supplementary Figures/` folder; source `survey_map/survey_map_and_timing.png`).

Geographic coverage and timing of the 124 DHS and MIS surveys and 47 MICS surveys with complete birth histories (41 countries). Circles are DHS and MIS surveys and triangles MICS surveys; filled symbols are the 99 DHS and 37 MICS surveys that contribute to the primary analysis (37 countries), and open symbols the 35 excluded surveys: 10 in Lesotho (5; malaria free, not processed), the Comoros (2) and São Tomé and Príncipe (3), which have no Snow et al. malaria-endemic polygon, and 25 for which a required covariate was unavailable for every survey × unit area after the declared substitutions (14 DHS, all 5 MIS and 6 MICS surveys; for each, the first missing covariate is listed in the primary's complete_case_losses.csv). Country shading indicates the number of included surveys; grey countries have none. Timeline rows are grouped by UN sub-region (Central Africa: UN Middle Africa), and n is the number of mothers (complete birth histories) contributing at least one child to the primary analysis, summed over the country's included surveys (1,125,610 in total). Where a MICS survey shares a country and year with a DHS or MIS survey (SEN 2015), the MICS symbol is drawn slightly above the line. The 46 MICS surveys without a complete birth history are not shown.

Country outlines are dissolved from the most recent available boundary file in each country (DHS files, or the analysis-region polygons built for MICS). Survey year describes fieldwork, not the calendar year assigned to each child's band entry. Per-survey exclusion reasons are in survey_timeline_all.csv.
