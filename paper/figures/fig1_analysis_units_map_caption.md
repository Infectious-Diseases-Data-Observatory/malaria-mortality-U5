# Figure 1 caption

Complete birth histories used in the primary analysis, by sub-national polygon of Snow et al. (2017). Fill: interviewed mothers with at least one child-band record in the analysis, summed over the 136 included surveys (99 DHS and 37 MICS; interviews 2003–2025) in 37 countries; in total 1,126,610 mothers contribute 7,800,785 child-band records and 108,657 deaths. Records are placed in the polygon holding at least 90% of the GPW 2020 population of their reported area, otherwise in the malaria-endemic polygons each holding at least 5% (malaria-free parts are left out, and areas more than half malaria free are dropped). In 215 of the 1,452 survey × area units this gives a union of two or more polygons; the 161,318 mothers (14.3%) in these unions are divided among their polygons by population share. The 465 polygons with data hold a median of 1,722 mothers (range 76–49,824) from a median of 4 surveys (range 1–11).

Light grey: the 55 malaria-endemic polygons without data, 53 in the 6 countries that have malaria-endemic polygons but no included survey (Botswana, Djibouti, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Somalia and Sudan), 1 in Mauritania and 1 in Namibia. Warm grey: the 29 malaria-free and 2 island polygons (Bioko, Annobón), not analysed. Pale: North Africa and Western Sahara, and islands without a polygon such as the Comoros and Zanzibar. Light blue: the 38 lakes and reservoirs of at least 300 km² within the map in Natural Earth (1:10m), drawn over the polygons and national borders. Slivers between the polygons and the national boundaries take the class of the adjacent polygon. White lines: polygon boundaries; black lines: national land borders of the sub-Saharan countries. Africa Albers equal-area projection (ESRI:102022); the scale bar is approximate.

Survey years, inclusion and exclusions by country are shown in the supplementary survey map and timing figure.
