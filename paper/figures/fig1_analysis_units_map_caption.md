# Figure 1 caption

Complete birth histories used in the primary analysis, by first-level administrative unit (polygons below): GADM 4.1 admin-1 units (KEMRI-Wellcome merge; national boundaries for Botswana, Ghana, Kenya, Madagascar, Malawi, mainland Tanzania and Uganda). Fill: interviewed mothers with at least one child-band record in the analysis, summed over the 136 included surveys (99 DHS and 37 MICS; interviews 2003–2025) in 37 countries; in total 1,127,215 mothers contribute 7,802,127 child-band records and 108,747 deaths. Records are placed in the polygon holding at least 90% of the GPW 2020 population of their reported area, otherwise in the malaria-endemic polygons each holding at least 5% (malaria-free parts are left out, and areas more than half malaria free are dropped). In 54 of the 1,634 survey × area units this gives a union of two or more polygons; the 33,815 mothers (3.0%) in these unions are divided among their polygons by population share. The 476 polygons with data hold a median of 1,686 mothers (range 76–50,029) from a median of 4 surveys (range 1–11).

Light grey: the 59 malaria-endemic polygons without data, all in the 6 countries that have malaria-endemic polygons but no included survey (Botswana, Djibouti, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Somalia and Sudan). Warm grey: the 31 malaria-free polygons (more than half of their population in the malaria-free areas of Snow et al. (2017), or without a MAP PfPR estimate) and the 2 island polygons (Bioko, Annobón), not analysed. Pale: North Africa and Western Sahara, and islands without a polygon such as the Comoros and Zanzibar. Light blue: the 38 lakes and reservoirs of at least 300 km² within the map in Natural Earth (1:10m), drawn over the polygons and national borders. Slivers between the polygons and the national boundaries take the class of the adjacent polygon; the 2 larger gaps (over 1000 km², 22,992 km² in all) are pale. White lines: polygon boundaries; black lines: national land borders of the sub-Saharan countries. Africa Albers equal-area projection (ESRI:102022); the scale bar is approximate.
