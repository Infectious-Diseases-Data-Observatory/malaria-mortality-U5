Figures contain no title, subtitle or embedded caption. Axis labels, age/year facet labels and legends remain. File numbering here does not alter the order of figures in TeX.

## Figure 1 — complete birth histories used, by analysis unit

File: `fig1_analysis_units_map.png` (source `analysis_units_map/analysis_units_map.png`; values in `analysis_units_map/analysis_units_by_polygon.csv`).

Complete birth histories used in the primary analysis, by sub-national polygon of Snow et al. (2017). Fill: interviewed mothers with at least one child-band record in the analysis, summed over the 136 included surveys (99 DHS and 37 MICS; interviews 2003–2025) in 37 countries; in total 1,127,850 mothers contribute 7,807,872 child-band records and 108,796 deaths. Records are placed in the polygon holding at least 90% of the GPW 2020 population of their reported area, otherwise in the malaria-endemic polygons each holding at least 5% (malaria-free parts are left out, and areas more than half malaria free are dropped). In 215 of the 1,452 survey × area units this gives a union of two or more polygons; the 161,597 mothers (14.3%) in these unions are divided among their polygons by population share. The 465 polygons with data hold a median of 1,722 mothers (range 76–50,029) from a median of 4 surveys (range 1–11).

Light grey: the 55 malaria-endemic polygons without data, 53 in the 6 countries that have malaria-endemic polygons but no included survey (Botswana, Djibouti, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Somalia and Sudan), 1 in Mauritania and 1 in Namibia. Warm grey: the 29 malaria-free and 2 island polygons (Bioko, Annobón), not analysed. Pale: North Africa and Western Sahara, and islands without a polygon such as the Comoros and Zanzibar. Light blue: the 38 lakes and reservoirs of at least 300 km² within the map in Natural Earth (1:10m), drawn over the polygons and national borders. Slivers between the polygons and the national boundaries take the class of the adjacent polygon. White lines: polygon boundaries; black lines: national land borders of the sub-Saharan countries. Africa Albers equal-area projection (ESRI:102022); the scale bar is approximate.

## Figure 2 — hazard ratio of death by age band, relative to zero prevalence

File: `fig2_hazard_ratio_by_age.png` (source `hazard_ratio_by_age/fig2_hazard_ratio_by_age.png`; values in `hazard_ratio_by_age/hazard_ratio_curves.csv`).

**Figure. Malaria prevalence and age-specific all-cause mortality.** Hazard ratio of death in each age band at national PfPR[2–10] relative to zero prevalence, from the seven age-band models of the PfPR-ACM model. Lines are point estimates and shaded areas pointwise 95% confidence intervals (conditional on the estimated smoothing parameters). Curves are solid over the central 95% of each band's exposure distribution and dashed from zero to its 2.5th percentile (about 2%), where the zero-prevalence reference is reached by extrapolation. The attributable fraction at prevalence P is 1 − 1/HR(P). For example, at 40% prevalence the hazard ratio is 1.08 (1.01–1.14) for neonates and 2.30 (2.00–2.65) at 24–35 months.

## Figure 3 — malaria-attributable fraction by age

File: `fig3_malaria_attributable_fraction_by_age.png` (source `attributable_fraction_by_age.png`).

PfPR-ACM model: estimated share of all-cause deaths attributable to malaria within each completed-month age band, at MAP PfPR[2–10] levels of 10%, 20%, 30% and 40% (lower panel), with the distribution of observed deaths by age band (upper panel). Age is drawn to scale; vertical lines mark the band boundaries at 1, 6, 12, 24, 36 and 48 months. Upper panel: each band's share of all under-five deaths observed in the primary sample, as bars of equal width at the band midpoints (bar width does not show band width). Lower panel: points show 100 × {1 − exp[f_g(0) − f_g(P)]}, using the seven separate primary MAP gamma=2 models, placed near the band midpoint; dashed lines connect discrete age-band estimates and do not imply a continuous-age fit. The denominator is all-cause deaths within each band, not all under-five deaths pooled across bands. The hazard-based attributable fraction follows the primary burden convention of holding annual person-time fixed; no country-specific IHME inputs are used here. Zero prevalence lies below observed exposure support, so the counterfactual requires extrapolation. Vertical bars show conditional pointwise 95% intervals, dodged by prevalence level; the interval limits and support flags are retained in attributable_fraction_by_age.csv.

## Figure 4 — national malaria-attributable under-five mortality with UN IGME all-cause mortality, 2000–2024

File: `fig4_malaria_mortality_rates_unigme.png` (source `mortality_rates/fig_malaria_mortality_rates_unigme.png`; the IHME version is the supplementary figure `sfig_malaria_mortality_rates_ihme.png`).

**Figure. Malaria prevalence and under-five mortality in 42 sub-Saharan African countries, 2000–2024, with all-cause mortality from UN IGME.** (A) National PfPR[2–10]: the Malaria Atlas Project's national estimates (release 202608; the same in both versions of this figure). (B) All-cause under-five deaths per 1,000 child-years, with crisis years replaced (below). (C) Malaria-attributable under-five deaths per 1,000 child-years: for each of the seven age bands of the PfPR-ACM model, the band's attributable fraction 1 − exp[f_g(0) − f_g(P)] at the national PfPR[2–10] times the band's all-cause deaths, summed over bands and divided by the same child-years. All-cause deaths are UN IGME estimates (UNICEF data warehouse, indicators CME_TMM0, CME_TMM1T11 and CME_TMY1T4) and child-years the UN WPP population under 5 (DM_POP_U5), the population UN IGME itself uses. UN IGME reports deaths at <1 month, 1–11 months and 1–4 years; each group is split across the model's age bands by the primary sample's observed deaths within the group, pooled over countries and years (1–5 months 52.4%, 6–11 months 47.6%, 12–23 months 37.9%, 24–35 months 32.2%, 36–47 months 19.0%, 48–59 months 10.9%). Crisis years, in which the UN IGME all-cause under-five death rate is more than 25% above its crisis-free trend (the log-linear interpolation between the nearest non-crisis years), take that trend in every age band, because the attributable fraction of crisis deaths need not resemble that of other years: Central African Republic 2009, 2014, 2017, 2019, 2021, 2022; Malawi 2001; Somalia 2011, 2022; South Sudan 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018 (689,289 deaths removed in all). Thin lines are countries, coloured by UN M49 sub-region (Middle Africa as Central Africa); the black line is the population-weighted average over the 42 countries (PfPR[2–10] weighted by the UN WPP population under 5; mortality rates weighted by child-years, that is total deaths divided by total child-years). The average malaria-attributable rate fell from 9.7 (95% uncertainty interval 8.5–10.6) to 3.3 (2.6–3.8) deaths per 1,000 child-years between 2000 and 2024 (27.1% and 20.9% of all-cause under-five deaths), a change of -66% (-71% to -63%). The grey band in C is the 95% uncertainty interval of the average, from 500 joint draws of MAP's national PfPR[2–10], the all-cause deaths and the PfPR–death curves (Supplementary Methods); the change is computed within each draw. Panels B and C use the same axis limits in both versions. Zero prevalence lies below the observed exposure support, so the counterfactual requires extrapolation.

## Figure 5 — PfPR-ACM against cause-specific malaria mortality by country, 2024 (UN IGME, A; IHME, B)

File: `fig5_model_vs_cause_specific_2024.png` (source `model_vs_cause_specific/fig5_model_vs_cause_specific_2024.png`; values in `model_vs_cause_specific/figure_data.csv`).

**Figure. Malaria-attributable under-five mortality in 2024: PfPR-ACM model against cause-specific estimates.** Each point is a country; rates are deaths per 1,000 child-years under 5. (A) PfPR-ACM estimates using UN IGME all-cause mortality (y) against UN IGME (CA-CODE) malaria deaths (x), both over the UN WPP population under 5. (B) PfPR-ACM estimates using IHME all-cause mortality (y) against IHME malaria deaths (x), both over IHME person-years. The PfPR-ACM estimate counts all deaths averted if prevalence fell to zero (direct and indirect); the cause-specific estimates count deaths assigned to malaria as the underlying cause. The dashed line is equality; countries are coloured by UN M49 sub-region (Middle Africa as Central Africa), and those at 2.5 or more per 1,000 on either axis are labelled with their ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 codes. Spearman correlation: 0.91 (A) and 0.91 (B); the PfPR-ACM estimate is higher in 30 (A) and 26 (B) of 42 countries. Vertical lines are 95% uncertainty intervals of the PfPR-ACM estimates, from 500 joint draws of MAP's national PfPR[2–10], the all-cause deaths and the PfPR–death curves (Supplementary Methods); the cause-specific estimates are shown without their intervals. The lower bound exceeds the cause-specific estimate in 15 (A) and 12 (B) countries, and the upper bound is below it in 5 (A) and 5 (B). Intervals beyond the axes are truncated with an arrow: South Sudan in A (upper bound 14.2). CA-CODE takes malaria from WHO estimates in 13 low-burden countries; South Sudan's IHME all-cause mortality is its crisis-free value.
